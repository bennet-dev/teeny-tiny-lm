What is this?

Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT predict text by understanding words as numbers in space. This is called an embedding—each word becomes a point in a multi-dimensional space where similar words are close together.

The Key Insight: Real LLMs use hundreds of dimensions, but we can understand the core concept with just 2 dimensions. In this demo, we use "Fruity" and "Techy" as our two dimensions.

When you type a prompt, the LLM looks at the words you've used, figures out where you are in "meaning space," and predicts which word would naturally come next based on what's nearby.

    How Prediction Works

    Step 1: Parse the prompt

    We look for words in your prompt that exist in our vocabulary and get their embedding coordinates.

    Step 2: Compute context vector

    We average all the word embeddings together. This creates a single point representing "where" your prompt is in meaning-space.

    Step 3: Find the closest word

    We measure the Euclidean distance from the context point to every word in our vocabulary. Smaller distance = closer match.

    Step 4: Return the winner

    The word closest to the context vector becomes the prediction. This is the core of how LLMs choose the next token!

    Why "Apple" is Special

    Notice how "apple" sits right in the overlap between the two clusters? It scores moderately high on BOTH axes because:


    • It's a fruit (some fruitiness)

    • It's a tech company (some techiness)


    This is why context matters! "I want to eat an..." pulls toward apple from the fruity side, while "I bought this from the store..." pulls toward apple from the techy side. Same word, reached from two completely different directions.
